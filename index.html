What this button actually does. It takes every line on your takeoff, one at a time, and goes looking for it on each supplier's quote. For each one it answers four questions and writes down its reasoning: did they price this at all, is it the same product or something else wearing a similar description, is it the same quantity, and is it the same unit so the numbers can even be put side by side. That is the first pass. The second pass turns around and checks your takeoff against what the bid documents require, looking for anything the job needs that never made it onto your list. Nothing is scored, nothing is priced, nothing is recommended: it is a read of what each supplier actually sent you against what you actually asked for.

Run the comparison

Why it runs in so many small passes rather than one big one. Categories are kept apart so a copper line is never asked to match itself against a toilet, and each category is then walked 25 takeoff lines at a time. That is not caution for its own sake: the answer has to carry a row for every line with a verdict and a reason for every supplier, and asking for a hundred of those in one go runs the answer out of room part way through. If a chunk still comes back short, it gets split in half and retried on its own rather than the whole category being written off.

This is the screen that matters. Everything the tool could resolve on its own is already gone from this list. What's left is what needs your judgment, sorted worst first. Two kinds sit here: quote issues, where a supplier's line does not square with the takeoff, and takeoff gaps, where the bid requires something the takeoff has no line for. The second kind is the expensive one, because no supplier will ever flag it for you.

Ask about these quotes

Answers come only from what has actually been read into this tab: the takeoff, the extracted quote lines, the schedule, the bid requirements, and the comparison result. It will tell you when something isn't in there rather than guessing.

How to read this: every cell in the comparison carries a match badge. EXACT means the supplier's line matched the item outright. EQUIV means it matched a different description the tool believes is the same product. SUB means the supplier offered something other than what was asked for. MISSING means nobody found it on that supplier's quote. UNCLEAR means the tool would not commit, which is a deliberate answer, not a failure. The confidence figure next to a badge is the tool's own, and low confidence means go look.

The takeoff is the reference. Every row, every match and every exception is measured against it, and every comparison is stamped with the takeoff version it was run on. Upload a revision and the tool tells you what moved and which cleared exceptions have gone stale, rather than quietly invalidating work you already did.

Totals shown per supplier are the sum of what that supplier actually put on paper. They are not comparable to each other until the exceptions are cleared, because a cheaper quote is usually cheaper for a reason sitting in that list. The tool never picks a winner.
